The trip, day by day

Gigs · food · what's actually on

Wed 30 Sep San Francisco Big night

Landing, and the best night of the SF leg

You land at 15:55 after roughly eighteen hours in transit. Annoyingly, this is also the strongest night for gigs in the whole San Francisco stretch — four good shows, all on the same evening, and then Thursday and Sunday go quiet.

  • Show Me the Body + Lip Critic + Whispers + Truck Violence August Hall, 420 Mason St · doors 18:30 · from $40 · all ages

    The realistic one. August Hall is a two-minute walk from the door of the Spero, so you can check in, shower and stroll over — and walk back out the moment jetlag wins. NYC hardcore with Lip Critic's noise-electronics underneath. Calendar

  • Chat Pile + Soul Glo + Virga Regency Ballroom · 19:30 · from $32 · 20 min walk

    On paper the best bill of the five nights — sludge filth plus the most interesting hardcore band in America. On a landing day it's a big ask. Listing

  • JPEGMAFIA + redveil The Warfield · 19:30 · from $55 · 8 min walk

    Billed as the Experimental Rap Tour, which is about right for the clipping. and Saul Williams end of your listening. Calendar

  • Author & Punisher + Guiltless + Ex Everything Neck of the Woods, Inner Richmond · doors 20:00

    Industrial doom performed on self-built machines. Sits between Neurosis and Blawan, which is a narrow gap to sit in. Listing

Thu 1 Oct San Francisco

Lower Haight, then Oakland

Daytime: the record crawl. Groove Merchant and Vinyl Dreams sit two doors apart on Haight Street, and Amoeba is a twelve-minute walk further up. One Muni ride from the hotel and you can lose an afternoon happily.

  • M. Sayyid (Antipop Consortium) + Jel (Anticon) + Heavy Arts Ensemble Thee Stork Club, Uptown Oakland · 20:00 · $15–20 · 21+

    The sleeper pick of the trip. Antipop Consortium and Anticon are the exact lineage running from dälek through to Infinity Knives, playing a 150-capacity dive bar for twenty dollars. BART to 19th Street. Calendar

Fri 2 Oct San Francisco

Hardly Strictly starts, and it's free

Hardly Strictly Bluegrass takes over Golden Gate Park from today until Sunday. Six stages, no tickets, no wristbands, endowed in perpetuity by a late banker who wanted the city to have it for nothing. The name badly undersells the booking, which runs well past bluegrass. Expect the Haight to be rammed — use Muni, leave the car.

  • Deerhoof + Greyhound + Etch Bottom of the Hill, Potrero · $33

    Thirty years of art-noise and still awkward in the right ways. Bottom of the Hill is the best small room in the city.

  • Death From Above 1979 UC Theatre, Berkeley · $39.50 + fees

    Noisy dance-punk, edging towards legacy act. They also play LA on the 8th, so you get two goes at it.

Evening alternative for the design half of your brain: the opening reception at the San Francisco Center for the Book for a show of Betsy Davids' letterpress and artists' books, 375 Rhode Island St, free.

Sat 3 Oct San Francisco Book ahead

Letterform Archive, then breakcore

The Letterform Archive runs a collections tour, 13:30–15:00, led by the founder Rob Saunders. You handle the originals rather than looking at them through glass. Twenty-five years into a career in brand and print, this is the single best two hours available to you in California, and it needs booking. 2325 Third St, Floor 4R.

  • Igorrr + Secret Chiefs 3 + Violent Magic Orchestra Great American Music Hall · doors 18:30 · $32.50–38 · 10 min walk

    Breakcore spliced with death metal — Venetian Snares rewired through Mastodon, essentially. Best value of the run and the closest proper venue to the hotel. Calendar

  • Hamdi + Conducta + Sam Binga Regency Ballroom · 20:00 · 18+

    UK bass and dubstep. Sam Binga is the reason to go if you're there for The Bug rather than the drop.

Sun 4 Oct Oakland & Berkeley

Cross the bay

Give the East Bay a full day. 1-2-3-4 Go! in Temescal is the hardcore shop — Converge, Quicksand, Fiddlehead territory. Stranded on College Ave does the noise and post-punk end, Cone Shape Top on Broadway is electronic only and open Thursday to Sunday, and Econo Jam on Telegraph has thousands of records at a dollar or two. Amoeba Berkeley and Rasputin face each other across Telegraph, so that's free.

While you're over there: 924 Gilman Street is ten minutes from Amoeba Berkeley. Volunteer-run, all ages, tickets around eight dollars, and the most storied hardcore room in America. Their calendar doesn't go out this far yet — check it in late September.

  • Acid Mothers Temple + Magick Potion Bottom of the Hill · back in the city

    Japanese psych-noise, thirty years deep and still deafening. The Mogwai-adjacent option if you'd rather stay west.

Also on: the Castro Street Fair, the oldest of the city's street fairs, and the last day of Hardly Strictly.

Mon 5 Oct SF → Pismo Beach Long day

The coast road

250 miles. Google will tell you five and a half hours; allow eight or nine, leave by eight in the morning, and accept that you cannot do all of the below. Highway 1 is open end to end for the first time in three years — the last slide at Regent's reopened in January — but check Caltrans before you set off.

The four nobody tells you about. Elkhorn Slough at Moss Landing has the densest population of sea otters on the coast, in an estuary everyone drives straight past; pontoon and kayak tours run from Moss Landing Road and want booking. Garrapata State Park south of Carmel has unmarked turnouts, no facilities and the best cliff walking north of Big Sur. The Henry Miller Memorial Library is a bookshop in a redwood garden with an outdoor stage and genuinely radical stock. And Nitt Witt Ridge in Cambria is a house one man spent 51 years building out of abalone shells and car parts, five minutes from Hearst Castle and infinitely more interesting.

Eating. Phil's Fish Market at Moss Landing does cioppino in a shed on the harbour. Ruddell's Smokehouse in Cayucos is a shack doing smoked albacore tacos, five minutes off the road.

Dinner: Jocko's, Nipomo

125 N Thompson Ave, fifteen minutes inland from Pismo, open Monday to Friday from 16:00. Red-oak pit, enormous spencer steaks, zero pretension, and a room full of people who have been going for forty years. It is the meal of the road trip and Monday is one of the days it's open. Ring ahead for three.

Tue 6 Oct Pismo → Los Angeles

Down to Sunset Boulevard

Old West Cinnamon Rolls on Dolliver St for the drive out. Then two stops worth the detour: the Guadalupe-Nipomo Dunes, where DeMille buried the Egyptian set from the 1923 Ten Commandments in the sand and left it there — the Dunes Center in Guadalupe has the excavated sphinx fragments — and Los Alamos, one block of Bell Street with a very good bakery and an antique row, which is a far better lunch stop than Solvang.

Nothing on for gigs tonight. I checked every listing in LA and there's nothing in your lane, so make it a dinner night — Musso & Frank on Hollywood Boulevard opens Tuesday, has been going since 1919, and the counter is the place to sit.

One to avoid today: Cold Spring Tavern up San Marcos Pass is a genuinely great 1868 stagecoach stop, and it's closed Tuesdays and Wednesdays.

Wed 7 Oct West Hollywood

Highland Park, and the Stones Throw pilgrimage

Another dead night for gigs, so spend it properly. Highland Park is the best record cluster in the city — Gimme Gimme, Arroyo and The Artform Studio are all within a few blocks of North Figueroa. Poo-Bah in Pasadena is twenty minutes east and is the most important shop on the list: underground and abstract hip hop is literally its specialism, and it's where the whole Lootpack and Madlib circle came up.

End the day at Gold Line

5607 N Figueroa St, open until 2am Wednesday to Saturday. Peanut Butter Wolf's vinyl listening bar, sitting directly underneath the Stones Throw offices — 7,500 records from his own collection, McIntosh amps, Altec Lansings, and a strict no-laptops policy. Rappcats, the old Madlib-orbit shop up the road, closed at the end of 2024, so this is now the pilgrimage. Build the Highland Park day so it finishes here.

Thu 8 Oct West Hollywood Clash

Lakers, or a gig

The one basketball night available to you, and it's a straight clash with two decent shows.

  • Lakers vs Sacramento Kings Crypto.com Arena, 1111 S Figueroa St · 19:30 · NBA preseason

    The only Lakers home game in your window, confirmed on the released preseason schedule. Preseason means cheap seats and a half-serious game, which for a first visit to that building is no bad thing.

  • Death From Above 1979 + Rickshaw Billie's Burger Patrol The Belasco, DTLA · 19:00

    A beautiful room. Same band as the Berkeley date, so only relevant if you skipped that.

  • Soulfly + Nailbomb + Incite Whisky a Go Go, 8901 Sunset · $43.75

    Nailbomb is the draw and the Whisky is a walk from your hotel. Not the best show of the week, but the most convenient by a mile.

Fri 9 Oct West Hollywood

Three very different rooms

  • ScHoolboy Q — Blank Face LP, ten years on Hollywood Palladium · 20:00 · 20 min walk

    Not underground, but Blank Face is the good one and a full-album anniversary show is an occasion. He also plays The Novo on Monday the 12th if tonight is spoken for.

  • The Killing Floors + Street Walkin' Cheetahs + The Strains Permanent Records Roadhouse, Cypress Park · 21:00 · 21+

    A record shop, a bar and a hundred-capacity venue in the same building. LA punk, cheap, and exactly the format you'd want on a Friday.

  • Bart Skils — four-hour set Warehouse, location emailed on the day · 23:00–05:00 · $25–45 · 21+

    Driving Drumcode-school techno. More mainstream than the Saturday warehouse party below, same crew and format. RA

Sat 10 Oct → Santa Monica The birthday

Fifty

Also your changeover day, so do the move to the Delfina in the morning and get it out of the way — the Palladium is twenty minutes' walk from The Moment but twelve miles from Santa Monica, so you'll be taxiing back in either way.

  • Mastodon + Deafheaven + Alcest Hollywood Palladium, 6215 Sunset Blvd · 18:00 · from $53 · on sale

    An all-time top-forty band of yours headlining a landmark room, with a blackgaze undercard that would headline most nights on its own. There is no better birthday option in this city on this date. Tickets

  • DVS1 — four hours, all night · with Mary Yuzovskaya Warehouse, address sent two hours before · 23:00–06:00 · $25–45 · 21+

    Because the Mastodon doors are at six, this doesn't clash — it continues. Proper underground warehouse techno, the Blawan lane, and a four-hour DVS1 set is a real event rather than a booking. Metal at six, techno until six. RA

Sun 11 Oct Santa Monica Clash

The week's real dilemma

  • dälek Moroccan Lounge, 901 E 1st St, Arts District · doors 18:30 · ~275 cap

    The most on-the-nose booking of the fortnight. Confirmed on both the venue calendar and the band's own routing. Small room, so book it now rather than deciding later. Calendar

  • Loathe + Fleshwater + Prostitute Hollywood Palladium · 18:30

    Head-on clash with dälek, and it's a genuinely hard call: Fleshwater is the Deftones–Quicksand–Glassjaw nerve pressed directly, and Prostitute is in your last-twelve-months top twenty.

Daytime: Yoko Ono's Music of the Mind closes at The Broad today, so it's now or not at all. CicLAvia also shuts the downtown streets to cars entirely, 09:00–16:00 — an empty Broadway is a strange and good thing to see.

Mon 12 Oct Santa Monica

Last full day

The Getty is free, ten minutes up the hill, and the Meier campus plus the view back over the basin is worth as much as the collection. Record Surplus at 12436 Santa Monica Blvd is the only substantial shop on this side of town — a hundred thousand records, heavy on the bargain bins, good for a Wu-Tang and Public Enemy trawl.

Evening: ScHoolboy Q's second LA date at The Novo downtown. Note that Musso & Frank is closed Mondays; Dan Tana's on Santa Monica Blvd runs until 1am and is the right kind of red-booth, chicken-parm, no-nonsense room for a last night.

Tue 13 Oct LAX 20:10

Out

Manhattan Beach is south of the airport and makes an easy last stop on the way in — quieter than Santa Monica, good pier, and you're twenty minutes from the terminal. Bay Cities deli on Lincoln Blvd for a Godmother sub to take with you; order ahead online or lose forty minutes to the queue.

Records

Verified as trading · addresses for the map

San Francisco & the East Bay

  • Stranded Records1055 Valencia St, Mission · 11–19 daily

    The best shop in the city for your noise, post-punk and experimental axis. Retail arm of the Superior Viaduct reissue label, in the old Aquarius Records space. Start here.

  • 1-2-3-4 Go! Records420 40th St #5, Temescal, Oakland

    The Bay Area hardcore shop, and a label besides. Non-negotiable given what you've been playing this year.

  • Groove Merchant687 Haight St, Lower Haight

    Rare soul, funk and breaks — a producer's shop. This is the raw material end of Madlib and DJ Shadow. Hours aren't published anywhere reliable; ring 415-252-5766 first.

  • Vinyl Dreams593 Haight St · two doors from Groove Merchant

    All vinyl, all electronic: techno, dub, drum & bass, imports. The Squarepusher and Leftfield counterpart to next door.

  • Cone Shape Top4124 Broadway, Oakland · Thu–Sun only

    Electronic, ambient and experimental, with stock that rotates constantly. Direct hit for Aphex, Blawan, Clark and Zomby. Mind the limited hours.

  • Econo Jam Records2443 Telegraph Ave, Uptown Oakland · 12–18 daily

    Punk, noise, avant-garde, metal and dub, with thousands of records at a dollar or two. Note the address — one listing site has it wrong.

  • Thrillhouse Records3422 Mission St · 12–20 daily

    Volunteer-run, not-for-profit DIY punk shop with a free arcade machine. The closest thing here to a British basement record shop.

  • Amoeba Berkeley2455 Telegraph Ave · Wed–Sun, closed Mon–Tue

    Smaller and cheaper than the Haight branch, with different used stock coming off the student market. Rasputin is directly opposite.

Los Angeles

  • Poo-Bah Record Shop2636 E Colorado Blvd, Pasadena · Tue–Sun 12–18

    Trading since 1971 and the historic hub of the Pasadena scene that produced Lootpack, Madlib and half of Stones Throw. Underground hip hop is the specialism, not a section. Worth the trek from West Hollywood.

  • Headline Records7706 Melrose Ave, Fairfax · 12–20 daily

    Punk specialist, ten minutes from your Sunset Blvd hotel, with the best hardcore shirt and patch selection in the city.

  • Mount Analog2217 Hyperion Ave, Silver Lake · Fri–Sun only

    Esoteric and global electronic, experimental, reissues. The shop most likely to be holding something adjacent to The Bug or dälek. Saturday the 10th and Sunday the 11th are your only windows.

  • Gimme Gimme Records5810 N Figueroa St, Highland Park

    Twenty thousand LPs, owner-run since 1994, deep used stock. Hours unpublished — call 323-550-1878.

  • Arroyo Records5123½ York Blvd, Highland Park · Thu–Sun 12–20

    Punk and indie weighted, opened by a musician in the old Wombleton space. Ten minutes from Gimme Gimme.

  • The Artform Studio5611 N Figueroa St #2, Highland Park

    A record shop inside a hair salon, stocking the jazz and funk that got sampled, and the flagship for Adrian Younge and Ali Shaheed Muhammad's Jazz Is Dead label.

  • Going Underground356½ E 2nd St, Little Tokyo · 11–19 daily

    Big, broad and open every day — indie, punk, metal, hip hop and electronic under one roof. The reliable one.

  • Record Surplus12436 Santa Monica Blvd, West LA · 11:30–19 daily

    A hundred thousand records and a huge bargain section. Volume rather than curation, and the only real option once you move to Santa Monica.

Don't waste a journey

Rappcats, the Madlib-orbit shop next to the Stones Throw offices, closed at the end of 2024 — Gold Line is the replacement pilgrimage. Fat Beats' LA shop is retail-closed. Wombleton, which stocked UK and European imports and would have been perfect for your Fall and Wedding Present habit, has gone; Arroyo is in the space. In Sheep's Clothing's shop and bar are both closed. And Jacknife Records appears to have moved out of Atwater Village to Alhambra — check their Instagram before going anywhere near the old Glendale Blvd address.

For the designer

Things worth a detour after twenty years in print

  • Letterform Archive2325 Third St, SF · Thu–Sun · $10

    Book the Saturday 3 October collections tour with the founder. The two current shows close in September and the replacement's dates aren't confirmed — go for the tour, not the gallery.

  • Arion Press & M&H Type2 Marina Blvd, Bldg B, Fort Mason · showroom Tue–Sat 12–17

    America's last combined letterpress and hot-metal Monotype foundry. Public tours run second Thursdays and third Saturdays, which misses you — but the showroom is free and you can see the pressroom. Ring 415-668-2542 and ask nicely.

  • William Stout Architectural Books804 Montgomery St, Jackson Square · closed Sun

    Still the best architecture and design bookshop on the west coast.

  • The Hallidie Building & Maiden Lane130 Sutter St, then 140 Maiden Lane · free, on foot

    The world's first glass curtain wall, from 1918, with cast-iron fire escapes used as ornament — then five minutes away, Frank Lloyd Wright's 1948 shop interior with the spiral ramp he reused at the Guggenheim. Both a short walk from the Spero.

  • International Printing Museum315 W Torrance Blvd, Carson · Saturdays only, 10–16, $12

    The largest working collection of antique presses anywhere, run with live demonstrations. Saturday the 10th is your only shot and it's thirty minutes from Santa Monica — which does collide with the birthday. The LA Printers Fair, 125 letterpress vendors, is 17–18 October and you miss it by four days.

  • Lucas Museum of Narrative ArtOne Lucas Plaza, Exposition Park · closed Tue

    Opened 22 September 2026, so nobody you know will have been. MAD Architects' building, and a collection built on illustration, comics and poster art rather than fine art.

  • Schindler House (MAK Center)835 N Kings Rd, West Hollywood · Wed–Sun 11–17, $10

    Schindler's 1922 house, effectively on your doorstep for the first four nights. Book ahead.

  • Watts Towers10624 Graham Ave · tours Thu–Sat only, $12, cash

    Thirty-three years of one untrained man's structural invention, and the restoration is finally finished. No reservations, cash or cheque only.

  • LACMA5905 Wilshire Blvd

    Two reasons beyond the lamp posts: Zumthor's Geffen Galleries opened in April, a 300-metre concrete arc bridging Wilshire; and Alfonso Gonzalez Jr.'s show, built entirely from LA's hand-painted storefront lettering.

  • Eames House203 Chautauqua Blvd, Pacific Palisades

    Reopened after the 2025 fire. Tour dates release in the first week of the preceding month, so book in early September or you won't get in. Ten minutes from Santa Monica.

  • Hennessey + Ingalls300 S Santa Fe Ave, Arts District · 11–19 daily

    Art and architecture books. The Cahuenga branch has closed, so this is the only one left.

  • The Frolic Room6245 Hollywood Blvd

    A dive bar next to the Pantages, wallpapered in Al Hirschfeld caricatures. Worth a drink for the wallpaper alone.

And the case for moving the flight

Three more nights would buy you a lot

Stones Throw is thirty years old in October and throwing a three-day festival next to its own offices in Highland Park. Madlib, Quasimoto and Madvillain account for something like five thousand plays in your library — this is your label's birthday as well as yours. Stack a Lakers game, a letterpress fair with 125 vendors and a record fair on top, and the same three nights that get you the festival get you all four.

The costs are real: a Trailfinders flight change, three more nights of hotel, and a car you'd need to keep. But if it's ever going to be worth doing, it's the year you turn fifty. Worth a phone call to Aimee to price it before you rule it out.
